# Consumer Protection Law in India

## Consumer Protection Act, 2019

### Overview
Replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986. Significantly expanded scope, introduced product liability, mediation, and e-commerce provisions.

### Key Definitions

**Consumer (Section 2(7))**: Person who buys goods or avails services for consideration; includes online transactions. Excludes persons who buy for resale or commercial purpose.

**Defect (Section 2(10))**: Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming in quality, quantity, potency, purity, or standard.

**Deficiency (Section 2(11))**: Fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in quality, nature, manner of performance of a service.

**Unfair Trade Practice (Section 2(47))**: False representation, misleading advertisement, refusal to take back defective goods, non-issuance of bill/receipt, hoarding, spurious goods.

**Restrictive Trade Practice (Section 2(41))**: Practice that tends to bring about manipulation of price or conditions of delivery of goods/services.

### Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions

| Forum | Pecuniary Jurisdiction | Appellate Authority |
|---|---|---|
| District Commission | Up to ₹1 crore | State Commission |
| State Commission | ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore (original); unlimited (appellate) | National Commission |
| National Commission | Above ₹10 crore (original); unlimited (appellate) | Supreme Court |

**Time Limit**: Complaint must be filed within 2 years of cause of action. Condonable on sufficient cause.

**Procedure**:
- Complaint filed; opposite party given 30 days to respond.
- If not settled by mediation, formal hearing.
- Orders: Remove defect, replace goods, refund price, compensation, discontinue unfair trade practice, pay adequate costs.

### Mediation (Chapter V)
- Consumer Commissions may refer disputes to mediation.
- Mediation cells attached to each Commission.
- If parties reach settlement, Commission passes order in terms of settlement.

### Product Liability (Chapter VI)
First time codified in India.

**Product Manufacturer liable if**:
- Product contains manufacturing defect.
- Product is defective in design.
- Deviation from manufacturing specifications.
- Product does not conform to express warranty.
- Product fails to contain adequate instructions for correct use.
- Product fails to warn of dangerous use.

**Product Seller liable if**:
- Exercised substantial control over design/manufacture.
- Altered/modified product.
- Made express warranty.
- Seller knew of defect but still sold.

**Exceptions to Product Liability**:
- Product was misused, altered, or modified by complainant.
- Complainant knew of defect but still used.
- Product was in compliance with mandatory standards.
- Defect occurred after the product left the manufacturer's control.

### E-Commerce Rules
Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020:
- E-commerce entities must display accurate information about products/services.
- No manipulation of search results to promote own products.
- No fake reviews.
- Mandatory country of origin labelling.
- Sellers on marketplace must have grievance redressal mechanism.
- Grievance Officer must respond within 48 hours; resolve within 1 month.

### Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)
- Regulates matters relating to consumer rights violations, unfair trade practices, misleading advertisements.
- Can issue recall orders, refund orders, discontinuation orders.
- Penalty for misleading advertisement: Up to ₹10 lakh (first offence); ₹50 lakh (subsequent).
- Endorsers (celebrities/influencers) also liable for misleading advertisements.

## Legal Metrology Act, 2009
- Regulates weights and measures.
- Mandatory declarations on packaged commodities: net quantity, MRP, date of manufacture, name and address of manufacturer.
- MRP must be inclusive of all taxes.
- Penalty for violation: Fine up to ₹25,000 (first offence).

## Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (FSSAI)
- FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India) regulates food business.
- Licensing and registration of food businesses.
- Standards for food products.
- Penalty for unsafe food: Up to ₹10 lakh.
- Penalty for death due to unsafe food: Imprisonment up to life + fine up to ₹10 lakh.

## Key Case Laws
- **Spring Meadows Hospital v. Harjol Ahluwalia (1998)**: Medical negligence falls within consumer disputes.
- **Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta (1994)**: Housing boards and government authorities are "service providers."
- **Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha (1995)**: Medical services covered under Consumer Protection Act.
- **Sapient Corporation v. Puja Sharma (2019)**: Employer-employee disputes not covered.
- **National Insurance Co. v. Hindustan Safety Glassworks (2017)**: Insurance disputes are consumer disputes.
