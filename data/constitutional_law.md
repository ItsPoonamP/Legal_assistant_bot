# Constitutional Law of India

## Structure of the Constitution
- Adopted: November 26, 1949; Commenced: January 26, 1950.
- Longest written constitution in the world.
- **Preamble**: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic; Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.
- 25 Parts, 448 Articles, 12 Schedules (as amended).

## Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)

### Article 14 — Right to Equality
- Equality before law + equal protection of laws.
- **Reasonable Classification**: Permits differential treatment if (1) intelligible differentia and (2) rational nexus with objective.
- Prohibits arbitrariness (E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu, 1974).

### Article 15 — Prohibition of Discrimination
- No discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth.
- **Exceptions**: Special provisions for women, children, socially/educationally backward classes, SCs/STs (Articles 15(3) and 15(4)).
- Article 15(5) added by 93rd Amendment: Reservations in private unaided educational institutions.

### Article 16 — Equality in Public Employment
- Equal opportunity in public employment.
- Reservations for backward classes, SCs, STs permissible.
- 50% ceiling on reservations (Indra Sawhney v. Union of India, 1992).
- Creamy layer concept: Excludes socially advanced members of OBC from reservations.
- 103rd Amendment: 10% EWS (Economically Weaker Sections) reservation upheld (Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India, 2022).

### Article 19 — Freedom of Speech and Expression
Guarantees 6 freedoms (originally 7):
1. Speech and expression
2. Peaceful assembly
3. Form associations
4. Move freely throughout India
5. Reside anywhere in India
6. Practice any profession/occupation

**Reasonable Restrictions**: Sovereignty, integrity, security of state, friendly relations with foreign states, public order, decency/morality, contempt of court, defamation.

### Article 21 — Right to Life and Personal Liberty
"No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."
- Expanded by Supreme Court to include: privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017), education, health, livelihood, dignity, speedy trial, free legal aid, right against solitary confinement, right to die with dignity (passive euthanasia).

### Article 21A — Right to Education (86th Amendment, 2002)
Free and compulsory education for children 6–14 years.
Implemented by Right to Education Act, 2009.

### Article 22 — Protection Against Arrest
- Right to be informed of grounds of arrest.
- Right to consult legal practitioner.
- Production before Magistrate within 24 hours.
- Preventive detention laws have additional safeguards (Advisory Board, maximum detention period).

### Article 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies (Dr. Ambedkar: "Heart and Soul")
Right to move Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights.
**Writs**:
- **Habeas Corpus**: "Produce the body" — against illegal detention.
- **Mandamus**: "We command" — to compel performance of a public duty.
- **Certiorari**: To quash inferior court/tribunal orders for excess of jurisdiction/error of law.
- **Prohibition**: To prohibit inferior courts from exceeding jurisdiction.
- **Quo Warranto**: "By what authority" — to challenge assumption of public office.

## Directive Principles (Part IV, Articles 36–51)
Non-justiciable but fundamental in governance.
Key directives:
- Article 39(b)(c): Distribution of material resources; prevention of concentration of wealth.
- Article 44: Uniform Civil Code.
- Article 45: Free and compulsory education for children below 6 years.
- Article 47: Prohibition of intoxicating drinks.
- Article 48A: Protection of environment.

**Relationship with Fundamental Rights**: DPSPs cannot override fundamental rights (Article 13). However, laws implementing Articles 39(b) and (c) are protected from Articles 14 and 19 by Article 31C.

## Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, 42nd Amendment 1976)
11 duties including: uphold constitution, defend country, promote harmony, protect environment, develop scientific temper.

## Federal Structure

### Union List (List I): 100 items — Parliament's exclusive power.
Examples: Defence, atomic energy, railways, banking, insurance, currency, foreign affairs, inter-state trade.

### State List (List II): 61 items — State Legislature's exclusive power.
Examples: Public order, police, public health, agriculture, land, water, local government, state taxes.

### Concurrent List (List III): 52 items — Both Parliament and State Legislature can legislate.
Examples: Criminal law, civil procedure, marriage, contracts, evidence, education, forests, population control.

**Repugnancy**: In case of conflict on Concurrent List, Central law prevails unless State law received Presidential assent before Central law.

## Amendment Procedure (Article 368)
- **Simple Majority**: Adding/removing scheduled areas, creating new states.
- **Special Majority**: 2/3 of members present and voting + majority of total membership in each House — most constitutional amendments.
- **Special Majority + State Ratification (50%+ states)**: Federal provisions — distribution of legislative powers, Seventh Schedule, representation of states in Parliament, Article 368 itself.

**Basic Structure Doctrine** (Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 1973): Parliament cannot amend the constitution so as to destroy its basic structure (democracy, federalism, secularism, judicial review, separation of powers, rule of law, fundamental rights).

## Emergency Provisions

### National Emergency (Article 352)
- Proclaimed on threat to security of India by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion.
- Fundamental rights under Articles 19 suspended; Article 20 and 21 cannot be suspended.

### President's Rule / State Emergency (Article 356)
- Proclaimed on failure of constitutional machinery in a state.
- S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994): Subject to judicial review; floor test before dissolution.

### Financial Emergency (Article 360)
- Never proclaimed.
- Central government can direct states on financial propriety.

## Key Case Laws
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**: Basic structure doctrine.
- **Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)**: Article 21 — procedure must be fair, just, and reasonable.
- **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)**: 50% cap on reservations; creamy layer.
- **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**: Judicial review of President's Rule.
- **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)**: Right to privacy as fundamental right.
- **Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)**: Section 377 decriminalised (consensual same-sex acts).
