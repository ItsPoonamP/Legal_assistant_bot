# Tax Law in India

## Income Tax Act, 1961

### Residential Status
Determines what income is taxable in India.
- **Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)**: Global income taxable.
- **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**: Income received/accrued in India + income from business/profession controlled from India.
- **Non-Resident (NR)**: Only income received/accrued in India.

**Test**: Present in India for 182+ days in the financial year = Resident.

### Heads of Income (Section 14)
1. **Salaries (Sections 15–17)**: Wages, pension, gratuity, perquisites, profits in lieu of salary.
2. **Income from House Property (Sections 22–27)**: Rental income from buildings/land; standard deduction of 30%.
3. **Profits and Gains of Business/Profession (Sections 28–44)**: Business income after allowable deductions.
4. **Capital Gains (Sections 45–55A)**: Profit from transfer of capital assets.
   - Short-term capital gain (STCG): Asset held ≤ 24 months (12 months for listed securities).
   - Long-term capital gain (LTCG): Asset held > 24 months (12 months for listed securities).
   - LTCG on listed equity shares > ₹1 lakh: 10% (Section 112A).
5. **Income from Other Sources (Sections 56–59)**: Interest, dividends, lottery winnings, gifts.

### Deductions (Chapter VIA)
- **Section 80C**: Up to ₹1.5 lakh for PPF, ELSS, LIC, NSC, home loan principal, tuition fees, EPF.
- **Section 80D**: Health insurance premium — ₹25,000 (self/family); ₹50,000 (senior citizens).
- **Section 80G**: Donations to specified funds/charities — 50% or 100% deduction.
- **Section 24(b)**: Interest on home loan — up to ₹2 lakh for self-occupied property.
- **Section 80TTA**: Interest on savings account — up to ₹10,000.

### Tax Regimes (Finance Act 2020 onwards)
- **Old Regime**: Higher tax rates but with exemptions and deductions.
- **New Regime (Section 115BAC)**: Lower rates; most exemptions/deductions not available. Default from AY 2024-25.

### Tax Rates (New Regime, AY 2024-25)
| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹3 lakh | Nil |
| ₹3–7 lakh | 5% |
| ₹7–10 lakh | 10% |
| ₹10–12 lakh | 15% |
| ₹12–15 lakh | 20% |
| Above ₹15 lakh | 30% |
Rebate under Section 87A: Tax nil if income up to ₹7 lakh (new regime).

### TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)
Withholding tax mechanism:
- Section 192: TDS on salary.
- Section 194: TDS on dividends.
- Section 194A: TDS on interest (10%).
- Section 194C: TDS on contractor payments (1%/2%).
- Section 194J: TDS on professional/technical services (10%).
- Section 194H: TDS on commission/brokerage (5%).

### Advance Tax
If total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000, advance tax must be paid in instalments:
- 15% by June 15
- 45% by September 15
- 75% by December 15
- 100% by March 15

### Assessment and Returns
- Financial Year: April 1 to March 31.
- Assessment Year: Immediately following financial year.
- ITR filing deadline: July 31 (individuals); October 31 (audit cases).
- Belated return: Up to December 31 of assessment year.
- **Faceless Assessment**: Assessments conducted online without physical interface.

## GST — Goods and Services Tax

### Overview
Implemented July 1, 2017. Replaced 17 central and state taxes (excise, VAT, service tax, etc.).
Dual GST structure: CGST (Central) + SGST (State) or IGST (Inter-state).

### GST Rates
- **0%**: Essential items — foodgrains, fresh vegetables, education, healthcare.
- **5%**: Essential goods — edible oils, sugar, spices, tea, coal.
- **12%**: Processed foods, computers, mobile phones.
- **18%**: Most services, standard goods.
- **28%**: Luxury/demerit goods — automobiles, tobacco, aerated drinks.
- **Special Rates**: Gold (3%), rough precious stones (0.25%).

### Registration
Mandatory if annual turnover exceeds:
- ₹40 lakh for goods (₹20 lakh for specified states).
- ₹20 lakh for services (₹10 lakh for specified states).
- Mandatory regardless of turnover: Inter-state supply, e-commerce operators, reverse charge mechanism.

### Input Tax Credit (ITC)
Registered taxpayer can claim credit for GST paid on inputs used in business.
Conditions: Tax invoice required; supplier has paid tax; return filed; goods/services used for business.
Blocked credits: Section 17(5) — motor vehicles (personal use), food, health services, club membership.

### Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)
Recipient (not supplier) pays GST in specified situations:
- Supply from unregistered persons (in specified cases).
- Specified services (legal, security, transportation, renting of motor vehicles).

### Composition Scheme
Small businesses with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (goods) / ₹50 lakh (services) can opt:
- Pay fixed percentage of turnover as tax (1%–5%).
- Cannot claim ITC; cannot charge GST to customers.

### GST Returns
- **GSTR-1**: Outward supplies (monthly/quarterly).
- **GSTR-3B**: Monthly summary + tax payment.
- **GSTR-9**: Annual return.
- **GSTR-9C**: Reconciliation statement (turnover above ₹5 crore).

### GST Council
Federal body — Union Finance Minister + State Finance Ministers. Decides rates, exemptions, law changes. Decisions by 3/4 majority.

## Stamp Duty
- State subject; rates vary by state (typically 3–10% for property).
- Mandatory on specified instruments.
- Instrument not duly stamped: Inadmissible in evidence; can be impounded.
- Maharashtra: E-stamping mandatory.

## Customs Act, 1962
- Governs import and export of goods.
- Basic Customs Duty (BCD) on imports.
- Integrated GST (IGST) + Compensation Cess on imports.
- SEZ (Special Economic Zone): Duty-free zone for exports.
- Anti-dumping duty on goods sold below fair market value.

## Key Case Laws
- **CIT v. Vatika Township Pvt. Ltd. (2015)**: Retrospective taxation — principle of legitimate expectation.
- **Commissioner of Income Tax v. Sun Engineering Works (1992)**: ITC cannot be claimed on basis of manipulated invoices.
- **Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India (2022)**: GST Council recommendations are not binding on Parliament/state legislatures.
- **Skill Lotto Solutions v. Union of India (2021)**: GST on lottery tickets — upheld, not violative of Article 14.
