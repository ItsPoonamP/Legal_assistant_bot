# Contract Law Basics — Indian Contract Act, 1872

## Definition of a Contract
A contract is an agreement enforceable by law. It is formed when one party makes an offer, the other party accepts it, and both parties provide consideration (something of value).

**Section 2(h)**: "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract."

## Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

### 1. Offer and Acceptance
- **Offer (Section 2(a))**: A proposal made by one person to another to do or abstain from doing something.
- **Acceptance (Section 2(b))**: When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent, the proposal is said to be accepted.
- Acceptance must be absolute and unqualified (Section 7).
- Acceptance must be communicated to the offeror.

### 2. Consideration (Section 2(d))
- Something in return — an act, abstinence, or promise.
- Must be lawful, real, and not illusory.
- Can be past, present, or future.
- **Adequacy**: Courts do not question the adequacy of consideration, only its existence.
- **Exception**: Agreements without consideration are void UNLESS they fall under Section 25 (love and affection, past voluntary service, time-barred debt).

### 3. Capacity to Contract (Section 11)
Every person is competent to contract who:
- Is of the age of majority (18 years under the Indian Majority Act, 1875)
- Is of sound mind
- Is not disqualified from contracting by any law

**Minor's Contract**: Absolutely void ab initio (Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose, 1903).
**Unsound Mind**: A person of unsound mind cannot enter a valid contract. A person who is usually of unsound mind but occasionally of sound mind may contract during lucid intervals.

### 4. Free Consent (Sections 13–22)
Consent is free when it is not caused by:
- **Coercion (Section 15)**: Committing or threatening to commit any act forbidden by IPC, or unlawful detaining of property.
- **Undue Influence (Section 16)**: One party is in a position to dominate the will of the other.
- **Fraud (Section 17)**: Active concealment of facts, false representation, or deceptive conduct.
- **Misrepresentation (Section 18)**: Innocent false statement of fact.
- **Mistake (Sections 20–22)**: Error about a matter of fact essential to the agreement.

**Effect**: Contracts caused by coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation are **voidable** at the option of the aggrieved party. Contracts caused by bilateral mistake of fact are **void**.

### 5. Lawful Object and Consideration (Section 23)
Consideration or object is unlawful if it:
- Is forbidden by law
- Defeats any provision of law
- Is fraudulent
- Involves injury to a person or property
- Is immoral
- Is opposed to public policy

### 6. Not Declared Void
Certain agreements are expressly declared void under the Act:
- Agreements in restraint of marriage (Section 26)
- Agreements in restraint of trade (Section 27) — with exceptions for sale of goodwill
- Agreements in restraint of legal proceedings (Section 28)
- Agreements by way of wager (Section 30)

## Types of Contracts

### Based on Formation
- **Express Contract**: Terms stated in words (oral or written).
- **Implied Contract**: Terms inferred from conduct or circumstances.
- **Quasi-Contract**: Not a true contract; obligations imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment (Sections 68–72).

### Based on Validity
- **Valid Contract**: Fulfils all essentials.
- **Void Contract (Section 2(j))**: Ceases to be enforceable; not void from inception.
- **Voidable Contract (Section 2(i))**: Enforceable at the option of one party.
- **Void Agreement (Section 2(g))**: Not enforceable by either party.
- **Illegal Agreement**: Forbidden by law; collateral transactions also become void.
- **Unenforceable Contract**: Valid but cannot be enforced due to technical defect (e.g., lack of stamp).

### Based on Performance
- **Executed Contract**: Both parties have performed.
- **Executory Contract**: Performance is outstanding.
- **Unilateral Contract**: One party has performed; waiting for other to perform.
- **Bilateral Contract**: Both parties are yet to perform.

## Performance of Contracts (Sections 37–67)

### Who Must Perform
- The promisor personally, if the contract requires personal skill.
- Legal representatives, if performance does not require personal skill.
- Third parties, if the promisee accepts.

### Time and Place of Performance
- If time is specified, performance must be at that time.
- **Time as Essence**: If parties intended time to be essential, failure to perform at agreed time makes the contract voidable at the other party's option.
- If no time is specified, performance must be within a reasonable time.

### Joint Promises (Sections 42–44)
- All joint promisors must jointly perform unless one dies or becomes incapable.
- Any one of joint promisors may be compelled to perform (joint and several liability).

## Breach of Contract

### Types of Breach
- **Actual Breach**: Failure to perform at the time of performance.
- **Anticipatory Breach (Section 39)**: Party renounces obligation before performance is due. The other party may treat the contract as discharged immediately or wait for the due date.

### Remedies for Breach
1. **Damages (Section 73)**
   - **Ordinary/General Damages**: Arising naturally from the breach.
   - **Special Damages**: Losses in contemplation of parties at time of contract (rule from Hadley v. Baxendale).
   - **Nominal Damages**: Breach proved but no actual loss.
   - **Vindictive/Exemplary Damages**: Generally not awarded in contract (exception: breach of promise to marry, wrongful dishonour of cheque).
   - **Liquidated Damages vs. Penalty**: Indian courts award only reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount stipulated (Section 74).

2. **Specific Performance**: Court orders the party to perform the contract. Available when damages are not adequate remedy (Specific Relief Act, 1963).

3. **Injunction**: Court restrains a party from doing something.

4. **Quantum Meruit**: "As much as is earned" — reasonable payment for work done when contract is discharged.

5. **Rescission**: Contract is cancelled; parties restored to original position.

## Discharge of Contract

A contract is discharged (terminated) by:
- **Performance**: Both parties fulfil their obligations.
- **Agreement**: Novation, rescission, alteration, or remission.
- **Impossibility/Frustration (Section 56)**: Performance becomes impossible due to an unforeseen event (destruction of subject matter, death of performer, supervening illegality, outbreak of war). Note: Commercial hardship alone does not frustrate a contract.
- **Lapse of Time**: Under the Limitation Act, 1963.
- **Operation of Law**: Merger, insolvency, material alteration.
- **Breach**: Actual or anticipatory.

## Indemnity and Guarantee (Sections 124–147)

### Contract of Indemnity (Section 124)
One party (indemnifier) promises to save the other (indemnity-holder) from loss caused by the promisor's conduct or third party's conduct.

### Contract of Guarantee (Section 126)
A contract to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person in case of default.
- **Principal Debtor**: Person for whom guarantee is given.
- **Surety/Guarantor**: Person giving guarantee.
- **Creditor**: Person to whom guarantee is given.
- **Continuing Guarantee (Section 129)**: Extends to a series of transactions.
- Surety's liability is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor (Section 128).
- Surety discharged if creditor gives time to principal debtor, or makes arrangement, or releases surety.

## Bailment and Pledge (Sections 148–181)

### Bailment (Section 148)
Delivery of goods by one person (bailor) to another (bailee) for some purpose on a contract that goods shall be returned when purpose is accomplished.
- Bailee's duties: Take care of goods, not to mix bailor's goods with own, return goods.
- **Finder of Lost Goods**: Treated as a bailee (Section 71).

### Pledge (Section 172)
Bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise.
- Pawnor: Person delivering the goods.
- Pawnee: Person to whom goods are delivered.
- Pawnee can sell pledged goods on reasonable notice of default (Section 176).

## Agency (Sections 182–238)

### Creation of Agency
- By express appointment
- By implied conduct
- By ratification (Section 196) — retrospective effect
- By necessity (Section 189)
- By estoppel (apparent/ostensible authority)

### Agent's Authority
- **Actual Authority**: Express or implied.
- **Apparent/Ostensible Authority**: Principal held out as having authority.

### Sub-Agent vs. Substituted Agent
- Sub-agent: Agent's agent; principal not bound by unauthorized sub-agent.
- Substituted agent: Named by agent at principal's direction; principal directly responsible.

### Termination of Agency
- By revocation by principal (reasonable notice required)
- By renunciation by agent
- By completion of business
- By death, insanity, or insolvency of principal or agent
- **Irrevocable Agency**: Agency coupled with interest cannot be revoked.

## Key Case Laws
- **Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co. (1893)**: Advertisement can constitute a valid offer.
- **Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose (1903)**: Minor's contract is void ab initio.
- **Hadley v. Baxendale (1854)**: Rule for remoteness of damages.
- **Satyabrata Ghose v. Mugneeram Bangur (1954)**: Doctrine of frustration under Section 56.
- **Standard Chartered Bank v. Andhra Bank Financial Services (2006)**: Anticipatory breach and remedies.
