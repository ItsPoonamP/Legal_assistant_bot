# Family Laws in India

## Overview
Family law in India is largely personal law — governed by the religion of the parties. There is no uniform civil code, though debates around it continue.

## Hindu Law

### Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
**Applicability**: Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, and those not Muslim, Christian, Parsi, or Jewish.

**Conditions for Valid Marriage (Section 5)**:
- Neither party has a living spouse at the time of marriage (monogamy).
- Neither party is of unsound mind or suffering from mental disorder making them unfit for marriage.
- Bridegroom is at least 21 years; bride is at least 18 years.
- Parties are not within prohibited degrees of relationship (unless custom permits).

**Registration**: Compulsory in many states following the Supreme Court's direction in Seema v. Ashwani Kumar (2006).

**Void Marriages (Section 11)**: Bigamous marriages, marriages within prohibited degrees (without custom), sapinda marriages (without custom).

**Voidable Marriages (Section 12)**: Impotence, unsound mind, consent by fraud/force, pregnancy by another man.

**Grounds for Divorce (Section 13)**:
- Adultery
- Cruelty (physical or mental)
- Desertion for 2 years
- Conversion to another religion
- Unsoundness of mind (3+ years)
- Leprosy or venereal disease
- Renunciation of the world
- Not heard alive for 7 years

**Mutual Consent Divorce (Section 13B)**: Parties living separately for 1 year; cooling-off period of 6 months (waivable by Supreme Court in Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur, 2017).

**Maintenance (Section 25)**: Either spouse may claim permanent alimony based on income, property, and conduct.

**Custody (Section 26)**: Best interests of the child standard. Joint custody increasingly granted.

### Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (Amended 2005)
- **Coparcenary**: By birth, daughters now have equal rights as sons (2005 Amendment).
- **Class I Heirs**: Son, daughter, widow, mother, son/daughter/widow of a predeceased son, etc.
- **Class II Heirs**: Father, siblings, etc. — inherit only if no Class I heir exists.
- **Mitakshara vs. Dayabhaga**: Mitakshara (most of India) — joint family property; Dayabhaga (Bengal/Assam) — no right by birth.
- **Stridhan**: Property of a woman — she has absolute ownership.

### Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956
- A Hindu male may adopt with wife's consent.
- A Hindu female may adopt if unmarried, divorced, or widowed.
- Adopted child has same rights as a natural child.
- Maintenance: Obligatory for wife, minor children, aged parents.

## Muslim Law

### Sources
- Quran, Hadith (Sunnah), Ijma (consensus), Qiyas (analogy).
- Applies to Muslims in personal matters; not codified (except some statutory modifications).

### Marriage (Nikah)
- A civil contract requiring offer (ijab), acceptance (qubul), and witnesses (two male or one male + two female).
- **Mahr (Dower)**: Mandatory payment/property promised to wife — prompt mahr (immediately due) and deferred mahr (on divorce/death).
- **Polygamy**: A Muslim male may have up to 4 wives, subject to equal treatment (courts have scrutinised this).
- **Void Marriages**: Marriage during iddah, marriage with prohibited relations, marriage without witnesses.
- **Irregular (Fasid) Marriages**: Without proper witnesses, in violation of number limitation — not void but needs rectification.

### Divorce
- **Talaq**: Husband's right to divorce by pronouncement. Triple talaq (talaq-ul-biddat) declared unconstitutional in Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017); Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019 criminalises instant triple talaq.
- **Talaq-ul-Ahsan**: One pronouncement + 3 month iddah period — revocable.
- **Talaq-ul-Hasan**: Three pronouncements over 3 months — irrevocable after third.
- **Khula**: Wife-initiated divorce with return of mahr.
- **Mubarat**: Mutual divorce.
- **Faskh**: Judicial divorce — Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986 (as amended) governs maintenance post-divorce.

### Maintenance
- Shah Bano case (1985): Supreme Court held Muslim divorced women entitled to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC.
- Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986 modified this.
- Daniel Latifi v. Union of India (2001): Reasonable provision for entire life or until remarriage, not just iddah period.

### Inheritance
- Fixed fractional shares (Quranic heirs).
- Males generally receive double the share of females in same category.
- Non-Muslims cannot inherit from Muslims and vice versa under traditional Muslim law.
- Waqf: Dedication of property for religious/charitable purposes.

## Christian Law

### Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (Amended 2001)
- Applies to Christians.
- Grounds for divorce: Adultery, conversion, unsound mind (5 years), leprosy, venereal disease, desertion (2 years), cruelty.
- Mutual consent divorce available.

### Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872
Governs solemnization of marriages.

## Parsi Law

### Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936
- Monogamy required.
- Marriage must be solemnized by a Parsi priest.
- Grounds for divorce similar to Hindu law.

## Special Marriage Act, 1954
- Applies to marriages between persons of any religion or nationality.
- Civil marriage performed before a Marriage Officer.
- Provides for registration of existing marriages.
- Divorce provisions similar to Hindu Marriage Act.
- Succession governed by Indian Succession Act, 1925 (not personal law).

## Domestic Violence — Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005
- Protects women from domestic violence (physical, sexual, verbal, emotional, economic).
- Covers wives, live-in partners, female relatives.
- Remedies: Protection orders, residence orders, monetary relief, custody orders.
- Domestic Incident Report filed by Protection Officer.

## Child Custody and Guardianship

### Guardians and Wards Act, 1890
- Court appoints guardian based on welfare of minor.
- Best interest of child is paramount.
- Natural guardian preferred unless unfit.

### Hindu Guardianship
- Father is natural guardian; after him, mother.
- For children below 5 years, custody ordinarily with mother.

## Maintenance Under CrPC / BNSS

### Section 125 CrPC (Section 144 BNSS)
- Applies to all persons regardless of religion.
- Wife, minor children, parents may claim maintenance.
- Maintenance may be up to what is just and proper.
- Interim maintenance available pending final order.

## Key Case Laws
- **Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017)**: Triple talaq unconstitutional.
- **Shah Bano Begum v. Mohd. Ahmed Khan (1985)**: Maintenance rights of divorced Muslim women.
- **Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020)**: Daughter's coparcenary rights under HSA apply even if father died before 2005 amendment.
- **Githa Hariharan v. RBI (1999)**: Mother can be natural guardian even when father is alive.
- **Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021**: Allows altruistic surrogacy; commercial surrogacy banned.
