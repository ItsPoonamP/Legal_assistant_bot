# Technology and Intellectual Property Laws in India

## Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act)

### Scope
Provides legal recognition for electronic transactions, digital signatures, and cybercrimes. Amended significantly in 2008.

### Digital Signatures and Electronic Records
- **Electronic Record (Section 2(1)(t))**: Data generated, sent, received, stored in electronic form.
- **Digital Signature**: Authentication of electronic record using asymmetric cryptosystem.
- **Electronic Signature**: Broader than digital signature; includes other authentication methods.
- Certifying Authorities (CAs) issue Digital Signature Certificates.

### Cybercrimes (Sections 43–74)

| Section | Offence | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| 43 | Unauthorised access, damage to computer systems | Compensation (civil liability) |
| 65 | Tampering with source code | Up to 3 years + fine up to ₹2 lakh |
| 66 | Hacking / computer-related offences | Up to 3 years + fine up to ₹5 lakh |
| 66A (struck down) | Offensive online messages | Declared unconstitutional (Shreya Singhal, 2015) |
| 66B | Receiving stolen computer resources | Up to 3 years + fine |
| 66C | Identity theft | Up to 3 years + fine up to ₹1 lakh |
| 66D | Cheating by impersonation using computer | Up to 3 years + fine up to ₹1 lakh |
| 66E | Violation of privacy (capturing private images) | Up to 3 years + fine up to ₹2 lakh |
| 66F | Cyber terrorism | Life imprisonment |
| 67 | Publishing obscene material online | First offence: 3 years; subsequent: 5 years |
| 67A | Publishing sexually explicit material | Up to 5 years |
| 67B | Child pornography | Up to 7 years |
| 72 | Breach of confidentiality by intermediary | Up to 2 years + fine |

### Intermediary Liability (Section 79)
- Intermediary not liable for third-party content if it acts as a mere conduit.
- Safe harbour available if: no initiation/selection of content, no knowledge of unlawful content, expeditious removal on notice.
- **Due Diligence Requirements** (IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021):
  - Publish privacy policy and T&C.
  - Appoint Grievance Officer (Indian resident).
  - Significant Social Media Intermediaries (5 million+ users): Additional obligations — Chief Compliance Officer, Nodal Contact Person, monthly compliance report, traceability of originator.

### CERT-In (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team)
- National agency for cybersecurity incidents.
- Mandatory reporting of cybersecurity incidents within 6 hours (2022 Amendment).
- Organisations must maintain logs for 180 days.

### IT (Amendment) Act, 2008 — Key Additions
- Section 66A (online speech, later struck down).
- Section 66F — cyber terrorism.
- Section 43A — compensation for failure to protect sensitive personal data.

## Data Protection

### Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA)
India's comprehensive data protection law (enforcement pending rules).

**Key Definitions**:
- **Personal Data**: Data about an identifiable individual.
- **Data Fiduciary**: Entity determining purpose and means of processing.
- **Data Principal**: Individual whose data is processed.
- **Consent Manager**: Entity managing consents on behalf of data principals.

**Rights of Data Principal**:
1. Right to access information about data processing.
2. Right to correction and erasure.
3. Right to grievance redressal.
4. Right to nominate a nominee.

**Obligations of Data Fiduciary**:
- Process data only for lawful purpose with consent (or other legitimate bases).
- Ensure data accuracy and completeness.
- Implement security safeguards.
- Notify data breach to Board and affected individuals.
- Erase data when purpose is served.

**Significant Data Fiduciaries**: Additional obligations — data protection impact assessment, data audits, data protection officer.

**Penalties**: Up to ₹250 crore per breach type; up to ₹10,000 for data principal failing to comply with duties.

**Cross-border transfers**: Allowed to countries notified by central government (whitelist approach).

## Intellectual Property

### Copyright Act, 1957

**What is Protected**: Original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works; cinematograph films; sound recordings; computer programs (treated as literary works).

**Ownership**:
- Author is first owner.
- **Work for hire**: Employer owns copyright for works made in course of employment.
- Commissioned works: Different rules for different types.

**Term of Protection**:
- Literary/dramatic/musical/artistic: Life of author + 60 years.
- Posthumous works: 60 years from publication.
- Cinematograph/sound recordings: 60 years from publication.

**Rights Granted**:
- Reproduction, adaptation, translation, public performance, broadcasting, communication to public.
- **Moral Rights (Section 57)**: Right of paternity (attribution) and right of integrity — survive assignment, cannot be waived.

**Infringement**: Doing any of the restricted acts without licence or beyond the licence.

**Fair Dealing (Section 52)**: Research/private study, criticism/review, reporting current events, judicial proceedings, educational use. India's fair dealing is narrower than US fair use.

**Digital Rights Management**: Protection of technological protection measures under the IT Act.

### Patents Act, 1970 (Amended 2005)

**What is Patentable (Section 2(1)(j))**:
- Novel (new, not publicly known anywhere in the world before filing).
- Inventive step (non-obvious to a person skilled in the art).
- Industrially applicable.

**What is NOT Patentable (Section 3)**:
- Mere discovery of a scientific principle.
- Discovery of a naturally occurring substance.
- Mathematical methods, mental acts, games.
- **Section 3(d)**: New forms of known substances not patentable unless enhanced efficacy proven — prevents evergreening of pharmaceutical patents (Novartis v. Union of India, 2013).
- Methods of treatment of humans/animals.
- Plants and animals (essentially biological processes).
- Computer programs per se (though software with technical effect may qualify).
- Business methods per se.

**Term**: 20 years from filing date.

**Compulsory Licence (Section 84)**:
- After 3 years from grant, any person may apply if:
  - Reasonable requirements of public not satisfied.
  - Patented invention not available at reasonable price.
  - Not worked in India.
- **Bayer v. Natco Pharma (2012)**: First compulsory licence granted for cancer drug Nexavar.

### Trade Marks Act, 1999

**Definition**: Any mark capable of being represented graphically and distinguishing goods/services of one person from another.

**Types**: Word marks, logos, shapes, colours, sounds, smell marks (not yet registered in India), certification marks, collective marks.

**Registration**:
- Application to Trade Marks Registry.
- Examination, publication, opposition period (4 months).
- Registration valid for 10 years; renewable indefinitely.
- Registered marks get the ® symbol; unregistered use ™.

**Infringement (Section 29)**: Use of identical/similar mark for identical/similar goods without authorisation causing likelihood of confusion.

**Well-Known Marks**: Special protection regardless of class (e.g., TATA, Google, Coca-Cola).

**Passing Off (Common Law)**: Protection for unregistered marks — need to prove reputation, misrepresentation, damage.

### Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999

Protects products associated with specific geographical origin.
Examples: Darjeeling Tea, Basmati Rice, Mysore Silk, Alphonso Mango, Tirupati Laddu, Kolhapuri Chappal.

GI tags valid for 10 years; renewable indefinitely.

### Design Act, 2000
Protects the visual features of a product (shape, configuration, pattern, ornament, composition of colours).
Registration valid for 10 years + 5-year renewal.

### Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act, 2000
Protects layout designs of semiconductor integrated circuits. Term: 10 years.

## Key Case Laws
- **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)**: Section 66A struck down as unconstitutional (free speech).
- **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)**: Privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21.
- **Novartis AG v. Union of India (2013)**: Section 3(d) upheld; Gleevec (imatinib) patent refused.
- **Bayer Corporation v. Natco Pharma (2012)**: First compulsory licence granted.
- **Amul Dairy v. Amulya Dairy (2007)**: Passing off for similar trade names.
- **Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak (2008)**: Copyright in judgements — selection and arrangement can be copyrighted, not bare judgements.
