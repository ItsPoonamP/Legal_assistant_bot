# Property and Real Estate Laws in India

## Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (TPA)

### Definition of Property Transfer (Section 5)
"Transfer of property means an act by which a living person conveys property to one or more other living persons, or to himself and one or more other living persons."

### Essentials of a Valid Transfer
- Transferor must be competent (major, sound mind, entitled to transfer).
- Property must be transferable.
- Transfer must be for a lawful consideration and object.
- Must comply with formalities (registration, stamp duty).

### Non-Transferable Properties (Section 6)
- Chance of heir apparent succeeding to estate.
- Mere right of re-entry.
- Easement apart from dominant heritage.
- Restricted interest (e.g., personal right).
- Right to future maintenance.
- Public offices, pensions.

## Sale of Immovable Property (Sections 54–57)

**Sale**: Transfer of ownership in exchange for a price paid, promised, or part-paid.
- For tangible immovable property worth ₹100+: Must be by **registered instrument**.
- For tangible immovable property worth less than ₹100 or incorporeal rights: Delivery of possession is sufficient.

**Rights of Buyer**:
- Charge for purchase money paid if seller cannot deliver title.
- Accrue to benefits if seller has been in possession.

**Rights of Seller**:
- Rents and profits until sale is complete.
- Charge on property for unpaid purchase money.

## Mortgage (Sections 58–104)

**Definition**: Transfer of interest in specific immovable property as security for repayment of a loan or performance of an obligation.

**Types of Mortgage**:

| Type | Description | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Mortgage | Personal liability; no delivery of possession | Suit for sale |
| Mortgage by Conditional Sale | Apparent sale with condition to retransfer on repayment | No possession transfer |
| Usufructuary Mortgage | Possession given to mortgagee to receive rents | No personal liability |
| English Mortgage | Absolute transfer with obligation to retransfer on repayment | Most common for banks |
| Equitable/Deposit of Title Deeds | Deposit of title deeds in notified towns | Popular with banks |
| Anomalous Mortgage | Any form not covered above | Combination of types |

**Right of Redemption (Section 60)**: Mortgagor has right to redeem at any time before sale/foreclosure.
**Clog on Redemption**: Any clause fettering the right to redeem is void.

## Lease (Sections 105–117)

**Definition (Section 105)**: Transfer of right to enjoy property for a certain time for a price (rent) paid or promised.
- **Lessor**: Transfers the right.
- **Lessee**: Receives the right.

**Types by Duration**:
- Month-to-month: Terminates by 15 days' notice.
- Year-to-year: Terminates by 6 months' notice.
- Lease for a fixed term: Terminates at end of term.

**Registration Requirements**:
- Leases for 1 year or more: Must be registered.
- Leases from year to year or for terms > 1 year: Must be in writing + registered.

**Rights of Lessee**:
- Quiet enjoyment.
- Deduct repair costs from rent if landlord fails to repair.
- Remove fixtures attached during tenancy.
- Subletting (unless prohibited).

**Lessee's Liabilities**: Disclose damage, pay rent, restore property on termination.

## Gift (Sections 122–129)

**Definition**: Transfer without consideration made voluntarily.
- Must be accepted during the lifetime of the donor.
- Must be made and accepted in donor's lifetime.
- Gift of immovable property: Registered instrument required.
- **Onerous Gift**: Gift subject to conditions/liabilities — donee must accept all or none.
- Gift cannot be revoked unless made under coercion, undue influence, fraud, or misrepresentation.

## Actionable Claims (Section 130)
Transfer of actionable claims (e.g., debt, beneficial interest in movable property) by written instrument and notice to debtor/trustee.

## Real Estate Regulation

### RERA — Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016

**Applicability**: Residential and commercial real estate projects above 500 sq m or 8 apartments.

**Key Provisions**:
- **Registration**: Every promoter must register project with RERA before advertising or selling.
- **Escrow Account**: 70% of amounts collected from allottees must be deposited in a separate escrow account; withdrawals only for land cost and construction cost with CA/engineer/architect certification.
- **Disclosure**: Project details, layout plans, approvals, timelines must be disclosed on RERA website.
- **Structural Defect Liability**: Promoter liable for 5 years for structural defects.
- **Possession Date**: Must be clearly stated; delays attract interest to buyers at SBI MCLR + 2%.
- **Agreement for Sale**: Standard format prescribed; no unilateral changes.

**Rights of Allottees**:
- Obtain stage-wise progress updates.
- Seek refund with interest for undue delay.
- Compensation for false information.

**RERA Adjudicating Officer**: Settles disputes between allottee and promoter. Appeals to RERA Appellate Tribunal, then High Court.

### Registration Act, 1908
- **Compulsory Registration (Section 17)**: Instruments of gift, non-testamentary instruments creating/assigning/limiting right in immovable property worth ₹100+, leases of 1 year+.
- **Optional Registration (Section 18)**: Other instruments.
- Registration confers notice and priority (Section 47 and 48).

### Stamp Act, 1899 / State Stamp Acts
- Stamp duty is a state subject; rates vary by state.
- Insufficient stamp duty makes document inadmissible in evidence.
- Instruments registered without proper stamp can be impounded.

## Landlord-Tenant Laws (State Rent Control Acts)
Most states have Rent Control Acts protecting tenants from arbitrary eviction.
- Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999
- Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958
- Tamil Nadu Buildings (Lease and Rent Control) Act, 1960
- Key protections: Reasonable rent, grounds for eviction (non-payment, personal use, subletting, nuisance), right to renewal.

## Land Acquisition

### Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 (LARR Act)
- **Social Impact Assessment (SIA)**: Mandatory for multi-crop and larger acquisitions.
- **Consent**: 80% consent of affected families for private projects; 70% for PPP projects.
- **Compensation**: Market value × multiplier (2× in rural areas, 1× in urban) + solatium of 100% = total compensation.
- **Rehabilitation and Resettlement**: Jobs, housing, annuity, or other benefits.
- **Urgency Clause (Section 40)**: Restricted use; compensation must still be paid.

## Key Case Laws
- **Suraj Lamp and Industries v. State of Haryana (2012)**: Power of attorney sales of immovable property not valid; registered instrument required.
- **Videocon Properties v. Dr. John Fernandes (2014)**: RERA's retrospective application to ongoing projects.
- **K.T. Plantation v. State of Karnataka (2011)**: Reasonable restriction on land use by state.
- **Pune Municipal Corporation v. Harakchand Misirimal Solanki (2014)**: Lapsed land acquisition; fresh acquisition needed.
