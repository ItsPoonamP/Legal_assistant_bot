# Criminal Procedure — CrPC 1973 & Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023

## Overview
The Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC) governed criminal proceedings in India. It has been replaced by the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**, effective July 1, 2024.

## Classification of Offences

### Cognizable vs. Non-Cognizable
- **Cognizable**: Police may arrest without warrant, investigate without Magistrate's order (murder, robbery, dacoity, rape).
- **Non-Cognizable**: Police cannot arrest/investigate without Magistrate's permission (assault, cheating, defamation).

### Bailable vs. Non-Bailable
- **Bailable (Schedule I, Column 5)**: Accused has right to bail. Police must release on bail.
- **Non-Bailable**: Bail is at discretion of court. No right to bail; court considers nature of offence, prior convictions, likelihood of absconding, threat to witnesses.

### Summons Case vs. Warrant Case
- **Summons Case**: Offences punishable with imprisonment up to 2 years — simplified procedure.
- **Warrant Case**: Offences punishable with more than 2 years — full trial procedure.

## FIR (First Information Report) — Section 154 CrPC / Section 173 BNSS

### Registration
- Cognizable offence → FIR must be registered.
- Non-cognizable offence → NCR (Non-Cognizable Report) only.
- If police refuse to register FIR → Complainant can approach Superintendent of Police or Magistrate.

### Zero FIR
FIR can be filed at any police station regardless of jurisdiction; transferred to the appropriate station.

### Key Supreme Court Ruling
**Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of Uttar Pradesh (2014)**: Registration of FIR is mandatory upon receipt of information disclosing a cognizable offence; no preliminary inquiry can be conducted before registration.

## Arrest

### Without Warrant (Section 41 CrPC)
Police may arrest without warrant if:
- Cognizable offence committed in their presence.
- Person has committed a cognizable offence.
- Reasonable suspicion of commission of cognizable offence punishable with ≥7 years.
- Person is a proclaimed offender.

**Section 41A BNSS (New)**: Mandatory notice for offences punishable up to 7 years; arrest only if person fails to comply or if necessary.

### Rights of Arrested Person
- Right to be informed of grounds of arrest (Article 22(1)).
- Right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner (Article 22(1)).
- Must be produced before Magistrate within 24 hours (Article 22(2), Section 57 CrPC).
- Right to inform a friend/relative (Section 50A CrPC, D.K. Basu guidelines).

### D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997) Guidelines
1. Arresting officer must bear identification.
2. Memo of arrest to be prepared and attested by family member or respectable local person.
3. Person arrested must be informed of right to have relative/friend informed.
4. Time of arrest and venue of custody to be recorded.
5. Medical examination of arrested person.
6. Copies of documents to be sent to Magistrate.

## Bail

### Section 436 CrPC (Bailable Offences)
Bail is a matter of right. Police or court must release on bail.

### Section 437 CrPC (Non-Bailable Offences)
Court may grant bail considering:
- Nature and gravity of accusation.
- Antecedents of accused.
- Likelihood of absconding.
- Safety of the community.
Bail generally not granted if accused is liable for death/life imprisonment (unless special reasons).

### Section 438 CrPC (Anticipatory Bail)
Bail granted in anticipation of arrest. Person can apply to Sessions Court or High Court.
**BNSS 2023**: Anticipatory bail to remain operative till end of trial unless modified.

### Section 439 CrPC (Special Powers of Sessions/High Court)
Can grant bail or impose conditions; can cancel bail granted by lower court.

## Investigation

### Steps
1. FIR registration.
2. Scene of offence — panchanama.
3. Examination of witnesses (Section 161 — statements not on oath, not admissible but can be used for contradiction).
4. Search and seizure (Section 165 — requires written record).
5. Arrest and custody.
6. Forensic examination.
7. Charge sheet (Section 173) — Police Report submitted to Magistrate.

**Section 173 BNSS**: Police must complete investigation within 60 days (extendable to 90 days); otherwise accused entitled to bail.

## Trial Procedure

### Commencement of Trial
- Cognizable offence → Police report to Magistrate → Magistrate takes cognizance.
- Non-cognizable offence / Private complaint → Complainant files complaint → Magistrate examines complainant → Issues summons/warrant.

### Charges
- Charge sheet read and explained to accused.
- Accused pleads guilty or not guilty.
- Guilty plea: Court may convict; must be voluntary and genuine.
- Not guilty: Trial proceeds.

### Examination of Witnesses
- **Examination-in-Chief**: Examination by party who calls the witness.
- **Cross-Examination**: By opposite party.
- **Re-Examination**: By party who called witness, on points arising from cross-examination.

### Statement of Accused (Section 313 CrPC)
Court examines accused after prosecution evidence; accused can explain circumstances appearing against them. No oath; refusal to answer or false answer can be considered as a circumstance.

### Judgment
- Acquittal or Conviction.
- Conviction: Court hears on sentence; considers mitigating and aggravating factors.

## Special Procedures

### Summary Trial (Section 260–265 CrPC)
Magistrates may try certain offences summarily (punishable up to 3 years) for quick disposal.

### Plea Bargaining (Section 265A–265L CrPC / Chapter XXI BNSS)
Accused may apply for plea bargaining after case is filed.
Conditions: Not applicable to offences punishable with death/life imprisonment or offences affecting socioeconomic condition, crimes against women/children below 14.
Process: Application → Prosecution + victim consultation → Mutually satisfactory disposition → Court passes judgment per agreement.

### Trial in Absentia (BNSS 2023 — New)
Courts can conduct trial in absentia for proclaimed offenders; judgment passed without presence of accused.

## Appeals

| Court | Appeals From |
|---|---|
| Sessions Court | Magistrate Court orders |
| High Court | Sessions Court orders; Magistrate convictions exceeding 7 years |
| Supreme Court | High Court orders; Constitutional questions |

**Section 372 CrPC**: Victim has right to appeal against acquittal or inadequate sentence.

## Key Provisions — BNSS 2023 Changes
1. Electronic/digital evidence expressly recognised.
2. Trial in absentia for proclaimed offenders.
3. Mandatory videography of search and seizure.
4. Handcuffing allowed in certain serious offences.
5. Forensic investigation mandatory for offences punishable with 7+ years.
6. 60-day deadline for charge sheet filing.
7. Judgments to be delivered within 45 days of completion of arguments.

## Key Case Laws
- **D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)**: Arrest guidelines; custodial violence addressed.
- **Lalita Kumari v. State of U.P. (2014)**: Mandatory FIR registration.
- **Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)**: Curbed unnecessary arrests in Section 498A cases.
- **Siddharth v. State of Uttar Pradesh (2021)**: Custody is not a must before filing charge sheet.
- **Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI (2022)**: Reiterated bail rights; default bail on failure to file charge sheet in time.
