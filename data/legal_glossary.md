# Legal Glossary — Key Terms in Indian Law

## A

**Ab initio**: From the beginning. A contract void ab initio is void from inception.

**Actio personalis moritur cum persona**: A personal action dies with the person (not applicable to most modern torts).

**Actus reus**: The guilty act — physical element of a crime.

**Ad idem**: Agreement between parties on the same thing in the same sense.

**Adjudication**: The legal process of resolving a dispute by a court or tribunal.

**Affidavit**: A written statement of facts confirmed by oath or affirmation.

**Aggrieved party**: A party who has suffered a legal wrong.

**Amicus curiae**: "Friend of the court" — a person who is not a party but assists the court with information.

**Anticipatory breach**: Repudiation of contractual obligations before the due date of performance.

**Arbitration**: Resolution of disputes by a neutral third party (arbitrator) outside the court system.

**Arrears**: Outstanding payments that are overdue.

**Attachment**: Legal process of seizing property to satisfy a court judgment.

## B

**Bail**: Temporary release of an accused person pending trial, usually on payment of surety.

**Bona fide**: In good faith; genuinely; without deception or fraud.

**Breach of contract**: Failure to perform a contractual obligation without lawful excuse.

**Brief**: A written document summarising the case and arguments presented to a court.

**Burden of proof**: Obligation to prove a fact; in civil cases — balance of probabilities; in criminal cases — beyond reasonable doubt.

## C

**Caveat emptor**: "Let the buyer beware" — buyer takes risk of quality unless seller fraudulently conceals defects.

**Certiorari**: Writ issued by superior court to quash orders of inferior courts made in excess of jurisdiction.

**Charge**: (1) In criminal law, formal accusation; (2) In property law, a form of security interest over property.

**Cognizable offence**: An offence for which police may arrest without a warrant (e.g., murder, robbery).

**Consideration**: Something of value exchanged in a contract; can be an act, forbearance, or promise.

**Contempt of court**: Willful disobedience of court orders or conduct that disrespects the court.

**Conveyance**: Transfer of title/ownership of property from one person to another.

**Coram**: "Before" — indicating the judge or authority before whom proceedings are conducted.

**Corpus delicti**: The body of the crime — the fact that a crime has been committed.

## D

**Damages**: Monetary compensation awarded to the injured party for loss suffered.
- Special damages: Specific quantifiable losses.
- General damages: Non-quantifiable losses (pain, suffering).
- Exemplary/punitive damages: Awarded to punish egregious conduct.

**De facto**: In fact; actually existing, even if not legally recognised.

**De jure**: By law; legally recognised.

**Decree**: The formal expression of a civil court's adjudication.

**Defamation**: Publication of a false statement injuring a person's reputation (libel in writing, slander in speech).

**Default**: Failure to meet a legal obligation, especially failure to repay a loan.

**Defendant**: The party against whom a civil suit or criminal charge is brought.

**Deposition**: Written testimony given under oath outside of court.

**Detinue**: Tort of wrongful detention of another's goods.

**Doctrine of Frustration**: Contract discharged when performance becomes impossible due to supervening event.

**Due diligence**: Reasonable care and investigation before entering a transaction.

## E

**Easement**: Right to use another's land for a specific purpose (e.g., right of way).

**Encumbrance**: A claim, lien, or liability attached to property.

**Estoppel**: Legal principle preventing a person from asserting something contrary to what they previously stated or implied.

**Ex parte**: Proceedings or orders made in the absence of one party.

**Executory contract**: A contract where obligations are yet to be performed.

**Expropriation**: Government acquisition of private property for public use.

## F

**FIR (First Information Report)**: The first report of a cognizable offence made to police.

**Fiduciary**: A person in a position of trust and confidence (e.g., trustee, company director).

**Force majeure**: Extraordinary event beyond parties' control excusing non-performance.

**Foreclosure**: Legal process by which a mortgagee takes possession of property after default.

**Forum shopping**: Practice of choosing the most favourable court for one's case.

**Fraud**: Intentional deception to gain unfair or unlawful advantage.

## G

**Garnishee order**: Court order directing a third party (garnishee) holding the debtor's money to pay the creditor.

**Good faith (Bona fide)**: Acting honestly and without fraudulent intent.

**Guardian**: Person legally responsible for a minor or incapacitated person.

## H

**Habeas corpus**: "Produce the body" — writ ordering a person detaining another to produce the detainee before a court.

**Hypothecation**: Pledging an asset as security without transferring possession (e.g., vehicle loans).

## I

**Immunity**: Exemption from legal proceedings or liability.

**Injunction**: Court order requiring a party to do or refrain from doing a specific act.

**In personam**: Legal action against a specific person (as opposed to in rem).

**In rem**: Legal action against a thing or status (as opposed to in personam).

**Intestate**: Dying without a valid will.

**Ipso facto**: "By that very fact" — automatically, by operation of law.

## J

**Judgment debtor**: A person against whom a court judgment has been given.

**Jurisdiction**: The authority of a court to hear and determine cases.
- **Pecuniary jurisdiction**: Based on value of dispute.
- **Territorial jurisdiction**: Based on geographic area.
- **Subject matter jurisdiction**: Based on type of case.

**Jurisprudence**: The philosophy and science of law.

## L

**Laches**: Unreasonable delay in asserting one's rights, potentially barring equitable relief.

**Lien**: Right to retain possession of property until a debt is paid.

**Limitation period**: Time within which a legal action must be brought.
- Contract suits: 3 years.
- Property suits: 12 years.
- Recovery of money: 3 years.

**Liquidated damages**: Pre-agreed amount of compensation for breach specified in the contract.

**Lis pendens**: "Suit pending" — notice that a lawsuit is pending over property.

## M

**Mandamus**: "We command" — writ directing a public authority to perform a duty.

**Mens rea**: Guilty mind — the mental element required for criminal liability.

**Mesne profits**: Profits received by a wrongful possessor during the period of wrongful possession.

**Mitigation**: Duty of the injured party to take reasonable steps to reduce their loss.

**Mortgage**: Transfer of interest in property as security for a loan.

**Mutuality**: Both parties bound in a contract (mutuality of obligation).

## N

**Natural justice**: Principles of fair procedure:
- Audi alteram partem: Hear the other side.
- Nemo judex in causa sua: No one shall be a judge in their own cause.

**Non est factum**: "It is not my deed" — defence that a person signed a document without knowing its nature.

**Notice**: Information given to a person about a legal matter. Actual notice vs. constructive notice.

**Novation**: Substitution of a new contract for an old one, with agreement of all parties.

**Nugatory**: Of no legal effect; void.

## O

**Obiter dicta**: Remarks made by a judge in passing, not essential to the decision; not binding precedent.

**Obligation**: Legal duty owed to another person.

**Onus probandi**: The burden of proof.

**Order**: Direction of a court that is not a final decree.

## P

**Parens patriae**: "Parent of the country" — state's role in protecting those who cannot protect themselves.

**Pari passu**: "At equal pace" — creditors rank equally.

**Plaintiff**: Party who brings a civil lawsuit.

**Power of Attorney**: Legal document authorising one person to act for another.

**Precedent (Stare decisis)**: Legal principle that prior court decisions are binding on lower courts.

**Prima facie**: At first appearance; on the face of it; sufficient to establish a fact unless disproved.

**Pro bono**: Legal services provided without charge, for the public good.

**Promissory estoppel**: A promise that the promisor should reasonably expect to induce action is binding even without consideration.

**Proximate cause**: The direct and immediate cause of damage (as opposed to remote cause).

## Q

**Quantum meruit**: "As much as has been earned" — payment for work done when no price is fixed.

**Quasi-contract**: Obligation imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment, without any agreement.

**Quid pro quo**: Something given in exchange for something else.

## R

**Ratio decidendi**: The legal reasoning or principle on which a court's decision is based; forms binding precedent.

**Rectification**: Court order correcting a written document to reflect the true agreement.

**Remand**: Sending an accused back into custody pending further proceedings.

**Repudiation**: Clear indication by one party that they will not perform their contractual obligations.

**Res judicata**: "The matter has been judged" — a matter once decided cannot be re-litigated.

**Restitution**: Returning the parties to the position they were in before the contract.

**Right in rem**: Right available against the whole world (e.g., ownership of property).

**Right in personam**: Right available against a specific person (e.g., contractual right).

## S

**Sine qua non**: An indispensable condition; essential element.

**Specific performance**: Court order directing a party to perform their contractual obligation.

**Statute of Limitations**: Law prescribing time limits within which legal proceedings must be brought.

**Sub judice**: "Under a judge" — matter currently before a court; parties should not prejudge.

**Subrogation**: Substitution of one person in place of another with reference to a legal claim.

**Summary trial**: Simplified and expedited trial procedure for minor offences.

## T

**Tortfeasor**: A person who commits a tort.

**Trust**: Arrangement where one person (trustee) holds property for the benefit of another (beneficiary).

**Ultra vires**: "Beyond the powers" — act done in excess of legal authority; void.

**Unjust enrichment**: Being enriched at another's expense without legal justification.

## V

**Void**: Of no legal effect from the beginning.

**Voidable**: Valid until one party elects to avoid it.

**Volenti non fit injuria**: "To one who consents, no wrong is done" — consent as a defence.

## W

**Waiver**: Voluntary relinquishment of a known right.

**Warrant**: Court order authorising police to arrest a person or search premises.

**Writ**: Formal written order issued by a court.
