# Corporate and Financial Laws in India

## Companies Act, 2013

### Types of Companies
- **Private Limited Company**: Min 2 shareholders, max 200; restricted share transfer; at least 2 directors; "Pvt. Ltd." suffix.
- **Public Limited Company**: Min 7 shareholders, no maximum; free share transfer; min 3 directors; "Ltd." suffix.
- **One Person Company (OPC)**: Single shareholder and nominee; introduced by Companies Act 2013.
- **Section 8 Company**: Non-profit; profits used only for charitable objects.
- **Nidhi Company**: Mutual benefit finance.
- **Producer Company**: Farmers/producers.

### Incorporation
- Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for all directors.
- Director Identification Number (DIN).
- Name approval (MCA portal).
- **SPICe+ Form**: Simplified Proforma for Incorporating Company Electronically Plus — single form for incorporation, PAN, TAN, GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC.
- Memorandum of Association (MoA): Objects, liability, capital clauses.
- Articles of Association (AoA): Internal rules and regulations.

### Share Capital
- **Authorised Capital**: Maximum capital a company can issue.
- **Issued Capital**: Portion of authorised capital offered to public.
- **Subscribed Capital**: Portion actually subscribed by investors.
- **Paid-up Capital**: Amount actually paid by subscribers.

### Directors
- **Board Composition**: At least 1/3 independent directors for listed companies; at least 1 woman director for certain companies; at least 1 resident director.
- **Managing Director (MD) / CEO**: Executive director responsible for management.
- **Duties (Section 166)**: Act in good faith for the benefit of the company, avoid conflicts of interest, not assign office.
- **Disqualification (Section 164)**: Adjudicated insolvent, convicted of an offence punishable with imprisonment ≥ 6 months, failure to file financial statements for 3 consecutive years.

### Meetings
- **Annual General Meeting (AGM)**: Within 6 months of financial year end; at least once a year.
- **Board Meeting**: At least 4 per year; at least 1 every 120 days; can be held via video conference.
- **Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM)**: For urgent matters.
- **Quorum**: For private company — 2 members personally present; for public company — 5 members.

### Accounts and Audit
- Financial year: April 1 to March 31.
- Mandatory audit by practicing Chartered Accountant.
- Filing of annual returns and financial statements with ROC (Registrar of Companies).

### Corporate Social Responsibility (Section 135)
- Mandatory for companies with net worth ≥ ₹500 crore, or turnover ≥ ₹1,000 crore, or net profit ≥ ₹5 crore.
- Spend at least 2% of average net profits of previous 3 years on CSR.
- CSR Committee of the Board required.

### Insolvency and Winding Up
- **Voluntary winding up**: By special resolution + declaration of solvency.
- **Compulsory winding up**: By NCLT order (inability to pay debts, just and equitable, oppression/mismanagement).
- **Insolvency Resolution**: Under IBC 2016 (see below).

## Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)

### Overview
Consolidated law for insolvency resolution of companies, LLPs, and individuals. Administered by IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India).

### Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)
- Triggered by: Default of ₹1 crore+ (default threshold enhanced during COVID: ₹1 crore).
- **Who can apply**: Financial creditor, operational creditor, corporate debtor itself.
- **Adjudicating Authority**: NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal).
- **Moratorium**: Automatic moratorium on all proceedings once CIRP admitted (Section 14).
- **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)**: Appointed immediately; manages affairs.
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC)**: Financial creditors vote on resolution plan.
- **Resolution Plan**: Must be approved by 66% of CoC voting share.
- **Timeline**: 180 days + 90 day extension; maximum 330 days (with litigation excluded).
- **Liquidation**: If no resolution plan approved or plan rejected.

### Individual Insolvency
- Fresh Start Process: For individuals with annual income ≤ ₹60,000 and assets ≤ ₹20,000.
- Insolvency Resolution Process: For other individuals.
- Bankruptcy Order: If resolution fails.

### Key Principles
- **Creditor in Possession**: IBC shifts control from debtor to creditors.
- **Waterfall Mechanism (Section 53)**: Priority in distribution — CIRP costs → secured creditors → workmen dues → employee dues → government dues → operational creditors → equity shareholders.

## Securities Law

### SEBI Act, 1992
- SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India): Regulator of securities markets.
- Powers: Registration, investigation, search and seizure, adjudication, penalties.

### SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015
- **Insider**: Person connected to the company with access to Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI).
- **UPSI**: Information about financial results, dividends, M&A, restructuring, etc.
- Trading while in possession of UPSI is prohibited.
- Penalty: Up to ₹25 crore or 3× profit, whichever is higher; imprisonment up to 10 years.

### SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011
- **Open Offer**: Triggered when acquiring 25%+ of a listed company.
- Acquirer must make public announcement to acquire minimum 26% from public shareholders.
- **Creeping Acquisition**: Can acquire up to 5% per year beyond 25% without open offer (25–75% range).

### SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018
- Regulates IPOs, FPOs, rights issues.
- Mandatory disclosures in prospectus.
- Lock-in periods for promoters and anchor investors.

## Foreign Exchange

### FEMA — Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
- Regulates foreign exchange transactions in India.
- Administered by RBI and Enforcement Directorate (ED).
- **Current Account Transactions**: Generally freely permitted.
- **Capital Account Transactions**: Subject to RBI permissions.
- **FDI (Foreign Direct Investment)**: Automatic route (most sectors) or Government approval route.
- **ECB (External Commercial Borrowings)**: Foreign currency loans by Indian companies.
- Violation: Civil offence (unlike FERA which had criminal provisions); penalty up to 3× the amount.

## Banking Laws

### Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
- Establishes and governs RBI.
- RBI functions: Monetary policy, currency issuance, banking regulation, foreign exchange management.

### Banking Regulation Act, 1949
- Regulates commercial banks.
- RBI can issue directives, inspect books, supersede boards.
- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**: % of deposits banks must keep with RBI.
- **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)**: % of deposits banks must maintain in liquid assets.

### SARFAESI Act, 2002
- Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act.
- Banks/NBFCs can enforce security (mortgage, hypothecation) without court order after 60 days' notice on NPA accounts.
- **NPA**: Non-Performing Asset — loan where principal/interest overdue by 90+ days.

## Competition Law

### Competition Act, 2002 (Amended 2023)
- **CCI (Competition Commission of India)**: Regulator.
- Prohibits: Anti-competitive agreements (cartels, price-fixing), abuse of dominant position.
- Merger control: Combinations above threshold require CCI approval.
- Penalty: Up to 10% of average turnover for 3 preceding years.
- **2023 Amendment**: Introduced deal value threshold for merger review (₹2,000 crore), settlement and commitment mechanism.

## Key Case Laws
- **Salomon v. Salomon & Co. (1897)**: Doctrine of corporate personality/separate legal entity.
- **Vodafone International Holdings v. Union of India (2012)**: Tax treaty, indirectly holding Indian company.
- **Swiss Ribbons v. Union of India (2019)**: IBC's classification of financial and operational creditors upheld.
- **Essar Steel India v. Satish Kumar Gupta (2019)**: Resolution plan binds all creditors including dissenting ones.
- **CCI v. Coordination Committee of Artists (2017)**: Collective bargaining constituting cartel.
