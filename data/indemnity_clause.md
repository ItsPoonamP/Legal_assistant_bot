# Indemnity Clause in Indian Law

## Definition (Section 124, Indian Contract Act, 1872)
"A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself, or by the conduct of any other person, is called a contract of indemnity."

**Indemnifier**: The party who promises to compensate.
**Indemnity-holder / Indemnitee**: The party who is protected.

## Scope Beyond Section 124
Section 124 covers only loss caused by human conduct. Indian courts have extended indemnity to cover losses from accidents and events beyond human conduct, following English law principles.

## Rights of Indemnity-Holder (Section 125)
When sued, the indemnity-holder is entitled to recover from the indemnifier:
1. All damages they are compelled to pay in a suit.
2. All costs they are compelled to pay in bringing or defending the suit (if acting prudently or with indemnifier's authority).
3. All sums paid under any compromise (if compromise was prudent or authorized by indemnifier).

## When Can Indemnity-Holder Claim?
**Controversy**: Section 125 says "when sued." Does the indemnity-holder need to actually pay first?

**Indian Position**: The indemnity-holder can claim indemnification as soon as their liability becomes absolute — they need not wait until they have actually suffered loss (Gajanan Moreshwar v. Moreshwar Madan, 1942, Bombay HC).

## Standard Indemnity Clause (Template)
"Party A ('Indemnifying Party') shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Party B, its officers, directors, employees, agents, and successors ('Indemnified Parties') from and against any and all claims, damages, losses, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees) arising out of or relating to:
(a) any breach of this Agreement by the Indemnifying Party;
(b) any negligence or wilful misconduct of the Indemnifying Party;
(c) any third-party claim arising from the Indemnifying Party's products or services;
(d) any violation of applicable law by the Indemnifying Party."

## Key Drafting Considerations

### Scope
- Define what losses are covered: direct, indirect, consequential?
- Include or exclude lost profits?
- Cap on indemnity amount?

### Triggers
- Breach of representation or warranty
- Negligence or wilful misconduct
- IP infringement
- Data breach / privacy violation
- Personal injury or property damage

### Procedure
- Notice requirement: Indemnified party must promptly notify indemnifier of any claim.
- Control of defense: Indemnifier typically has right to control defense.
- Cooperation: Indemnified party must cooperate.
- Settlement: Indemnifier cannot settle without indemnitee's consent if settlement imposes obligations on indemnitee.

### Caps and Floors
- **Cap**: Maximum indemnity liability (e.g., contract value, insurance amount).
- **Basket/Deductible**: Minimum threshold before indemnity kicks in.
- **Carve-outs**: Fraud, wilful misconduct, gross negligence typically uncapped.

## Indemnity vs. Guarantee vs. Insurance

| Feature | Indemnity | Guarantee | Insurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parties | 2 (indemnifier + indemnitee) | 3 (surety + creditor + principal debtor) | Insurer + insured |
| Nature | Primary obligation | Secondary obligation | Contingent obligation |
| Trigger | Loss from conduct | Default of principal debtor | Specified insured event |
| Proof of loss | Required | Not required (liability co-extensive) | Required + insurable interest |

## Mutual Indemnity
Both parties indemnify each other for their respective acts. Common in technology contracts and joint ventures.

## Broad vs. Narrow Indemnity
- **Broad Form**: Covers indemnitee's own negligence — disfavoured; courts may read down.
- **Intermediate Form**: Covers concurrent negligence of both parties.
- **Narrow Form**: Only covers indemnitor's negligence — most common.

## Tax on Indemnity Payments
Indemnity payments for capital losses are generally treated as capital receipts (not taxable). Indemnity for revenue losses or loss of profits may be taxable as income.

## Key Case Laws
- **Gajanan Moreshwar v. Moreshwar Madan (1942)**: Indemnity-holder can seek relief before actual payment when liability is absolute.
- **New India Assurance Co. v. State Trading Corp (1986)**: Scope of indemnity must be construed strictly against the indemnifier.
- **Irrawaddy Flotilla Co. v. Bugwandas (1891)**: Indemnifier liable for all losses falling within the clause even if unforeseeable.
