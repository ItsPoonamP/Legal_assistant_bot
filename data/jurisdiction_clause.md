# Jurisdiction Clause in Indian Law

## Definition
A jurisdiction clause (also called a forum selection clause or choice of forum clause) is a contractual provision specifying which court or tribunal will have authority to hear disputes arising from the contract.

## Types of Jurisdiction

### 1. Exclusive Jurisdiction Clause
Only one specific court has authority to hear disputes.
"The courts of Mumbai shall have exclusive jurisdiction over all disputes arising from this Agreement."
- Oust the jurisdiction of all other courts.
- Enforceable in India if the chosen court otherwise has jurisdiction (ABC Laminart v. AP Agencies, 1989).

### 2. Non-Exclusive (Permissive) Jurisdiction Clause
Specifies a preferred court but does not prevent other courts from hearing the matter.
"The parties submit to the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of Delhi."

### 3. Asymmetric Jurisdiction Clause
One party (usually the stronger party) has wider options.
Common in finance contracts — lender can sue in any jurisdiction, borrower restricted to one.

## Governing Law in India — Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC)

### Ordinary Original Civil Jurisdiction
Courts can hear suits based on:
- **Territorial jurisdiction (Section 15–20)**: Where defendant resides/carries on business, where cause of action arose, where property is situated.
- **Pecuniary jurisdiction**: Value of the suit determines which level of court.
- **Subject matter jurisdiction**: Certain matters reserved for specific courts.

### Section 20 — Where Suits May Be Instituted
A suit may be filed where:
(a) The defendant actually and voluntarily resides, carries on business, or personally works for gain.
(b) Any of the defendants resides, carries on business (with leave of court for other defendants).
(c) The cause of action wholly or partly arose.

## Ouster of Jurisdiction

### Can Parties Exclude a Court's Jurisdiction?
Yes, partially. Where two or more courts have jurisdiction, parties can agree to exclude one and vest it in another — this is valid as it narrows down options, not ousts jurisdiction entirely.

**ABC Laminart Pvt. Ltd. v. AP Agencies (1989)**: Supreme Court held that parties can by agreement confer jurisdiction on one of two or more competent courts and exclude others, provided the chosen court has inherent jurisdiction.

### What Parties CANNOT Do
- Cannot confer jurisdiction on a court that has no jurisdiction under the law.
- Cannot oust jurisdiction of all courts entirely.
- Cannot exclude the jurisdiction of a court that has exclusive statutory jurisdiction.

## Jurisdiction in Indian Civil Courts

### Hierarchy
1. District Court / City Civil Court
2. High Court (Original Side — only in certain cities)
3. Supreme Court (Appellate jurisdiction primarily; some original)

### Specialised Courts/Tribunals
- **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)**: Company law, insolvency matters.
- **Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT)**: Recovery of debts by banks/financial institutions above ₹20 lakh.
- **Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission**: Consumer complaints.
- **National Green Tribunal (NGT)**: Environmental disputes.
- **Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)**: Tax disputes.
- **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)**: SEBI orders.

## Choice of Law vs. Choice of Forum

### Choice of Law Clause
Specifies which country's/state's law governs the contract.
"This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of India."

### Choice of Forum Clause
Specifies where disputes will be heard. The two are independent — parties can choose Indian law to govern but seat arbitration in Singapore.

## International Jurisdiction

### Private International Law
In cross-border contracts, courts must determine:
1. **Jurisdiction**: Which country's court can hear the dispute?
2. **Applicable Law**: Which country's law governs the contract?
3. **Recognition and Enforcement**: Will the judgment be recognised abroad?

### Doctrine of Forum Non Conveniens
Court may decline jurisdiction if another court is a more appropriate forum, even if it has jurisdiction.
Factors: Location of parties, witnesses, evidence; governing law; interests of justice.

## Jurisdiction in Arbitration vs. Courts

### Kompetenz-Kompetenz
An arbitral tribunal has the power to rule on its own jurisdiction (Section 16, Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996).

### Anti-Suit Injunction
A court may restrain a party from proceeding in a foreign court or arbitration if doing so would be vexatious or oppressive.

## Service of Summons Outside India (CPC Order V)
- Service through diplomatic channels.
- Service through Indian courts under bilateral/multilateral treaties.
- Hague Convention on Service Abroad (India is not a signatory but bilateral treaties exist).

## Draft Jurisdiction Clause (Template)

**Exclusive Jurisdiction (Domestic)**:
"This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of India. The courts located in [City], India shall have exclusive jurisdiction over any disputes arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, and each party hereby irrevocably submits to such jurisdiction."

**Combined Arbitration + Courts for Interim Relief**:
"Any dispute shall be referred to arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. The seat of arbitration shall be [City], India. Notwithstanding the foregoing, either party may seek interim relief from courts of competent jurisdiction."

## Key Case Laws
- **ABC Laminart Pvt. Ltd. v. AP Agencies (1989)**: Parties can restrict jurisdiction to one of multiple competent courts.
- **Swastik Gases v. Indian Oil Corporation (2013)**: Exclusive jurisdiction clause valid; words "alone," "only," "exclusive" not necessary — context determines intent.
- **Modi Entertainment Network v. WSG Cricket PTE Ltd. (2003)**: Anti-suit injunction principles in India.
- **Cheran Properties v. Kasturi and Sons (2018)**: Non-signatory to arbitration agreement can be bound in certain circumstances.
