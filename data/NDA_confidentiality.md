# Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) and Confidentiality Law in India

## What is an NDA?
A Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA), also called a Confidentiality Agreement (CA) or Proprietary Information Agreement (PIA), is a contract between parties to keep certain information secret and not disclose it to third parties.

## Types of NDAs

### Unilateral NDA
Only one party discloses confidential information; only the other party is bound to maintain confidentiality.
Common in: Employee onboarding, vendor engagement, investor discussions (early stage).

### Mutual/Bilateral NDA
Both parties disclose confidential information; both are bound.
Common in: Joint ventures, M&A due diligence, technology partnerships, co-development agreements.

### Multilateral NDA
Multiple parties (3+) involved; at least one discloses to the others.
Common in: Consortium agreements, multi-party joint ventures.

## Essential Clauses in an NDA

### 1. Definition of Confidential Information
Most important and heavily negotiated clause.

**Broad Definition (Disclosing Party Prefers)**:
"Confidential Information means any and all information disclosed by one party to the other, whether disclosed orally, in writing, electronically or otherwise, including but not limited to: business plans, financial data, customer lists, supplier information, technical data, trade secrets, processes, formulas, algorithms, source code, designs, prototypes, research results, marketing plans, and any other information designated as confidential."

**Narrow Definition (Receiving Party Prefers)**:
"Confidential Information means only information that is: (a) marked 'Confidential' or 'Proprietary' at the time of disclosure, or (b) if disclosed orally, identified as confidential at the time of disclosure and confirmed in writing within 7 days."

### 2. Exclusions from Confidential Information (Standard)
Information is NOT confidential if it:
(a) Was publicly known at the time of disclosure.
(b) Becomes publicly known through no fault of the receiving party.
(c) Was already known to the receiving party before disclosure (provable by prior written records).
(d) Is independently developed by the receiving party without use of disclosing party's information.
(e) Is disclosed to the receiving party by a third party not under obligation of confidentiality.
(f) Is required to be disclosed by law, court order, or regulatory authority (with prior notice to disclosing party where possible).

### 3. Obligations of Receiving Party
- Keep information strictly confidential.
- Use information only for the Permitted Purpose (defined in agreement).
- Disclose only on need-to-know basis within organisation.
- Apply same standard of protection as own confidential information (minimum: reasonable care).
- Notify disclosing party immediately on becoming aware of any unauthorised use or disclosure.
- Not reverse engineer, decompile, or disassemble any products.

### 4. Permitted Purpose
The specific reason for which information is shared.
"Permitted Purpose means the evaluation of a potential business relationship between the parties relating to [describe project/transaction]."

### 5. Term and Duration
- **Agreement Term**: Period during which disclosures can be made (e.g., 1 year).
- **Confidentiality Obligation**: Duration of obligation to maintain confidentiality.
  - For general information: 2–5 years from disclosure or end of agreement.
  - For trade secrets: Often indefinite (trade secrets have no statutory expiry if maintained as secret).

### 6. Return or Destruction of Information
On termination or request by disclosing party, receiving party must:
- Return all confidential information.
- Destroy all copies (including electronic copies) and certify destruction.
- Exception: Copies required by law or automated backup systems (subject to continued confidentiality).

### 7. Injunctive Relief
Monetary damages alone are insufficient for breach of confidentiality (irreparable harm). Clause typically provides:
"Receiving Party acknowledges that any breach would cause irreparable harm and that disclosing party shall be entitled to seek injunctive relief without bond or other security, in addition to any other remedies at law."

### 8. No Licence
The NDA does not grant any licence, right, or interest in any intellectual property disclosed.

### 9. No Obligation to Disclose
Neither party is obligated to disclose any information; NDA merely governs what happens if disclosure is made.

## Legal Framework in India

### Trade Secrets
India does not have a specific Trade Secrets Act. Protection comes from:
- **Indian Contract Act, 1872**: NDA is a contract; breach is actionable.
- **Common Law**: Courts protect confidential information through equity principles.
- **IT Act, 2000**: Sections 43, 72 — unauthorised access and breach of confidentiality.
- **Indian Penal Code**: Sections 408, 420 — criminal breach of trust, cheating.

### Enforceability
- NDAs are enforceable as contracts if they meet all requirements of the Indian Contract Act.
- Consideration requirement: In employment NDAs, the job/continued employment constitutes consideration.
- **Restraint of Trade (Section 27 ICA)**: NDAs must not be so broad as to constitute a restraint of trade. Courts may strike down excessively wide or unreasonable non-disclosure obligations.
- Post-employment NDAs restricting disclosure of trade secrets are enforceable; those preventing use of general skills are not.

### Specific Relief Act, 1963
- Injunction to restrain breach of NDA available under Section 38.
- Specific performance of NDA generally not available (personal services).

## NDA in Employment Context

### Pre-Employment NDA
Signed before sharing information during interview/due diligence process.

### Employment NDA
Part of employment agreement or separate document signed at joining.
- Covers: Trade secrets, customer data, business strategies, product roadmaps.
- Survives termination.

### Limits in India
- Cannot prevent employee from using general skills and knowledge acquired during employment.
- Cannot prevent disclosure of illegal activities (whistleblower protection).
- Section 27 ICA limits on post-termination restraints.

## NDA in M&A / Investment

### Due Diligence NDA
Signed before target company shares financial, legal, operational data with potential buyer/investor.
- Typically unilateral (target discloses to buyer).
- Includes standstill clause (buyer cannot approach target's employees/customers directly).
- Often includes no-poach provision.

### Clean Team Provision
Where sensitive competitive information is shared, only designated "clean team" members can access — segregated from business decision-makers.

## Breach of NDA — Remedies

### Civil Remedies
- Injunction (interim and permanent).
- Damages — actual loss suffered.
- Account of profits — profits made by breaching party.
- Delivery up and destruction of documents.

### Criminal Remedies
- Section 408 IPC: Criminal breach of trust (up to 7 years).
- Section 420 IPC: Cheating (up to 7 years).
- Section 72 IT Act: Breach of confidentiality by intermediary (up to 2 years).

## Standard Short-Form NDA Template (India)

MUTUAL NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT

This Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of [Date] between [Party A] and [Party B] (collectively, "Parties").

1. CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION: "Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed by either party to the other, in any form, in connection with [Purpose], that is designated as confidential or should reasonably be understood to be confidential given the nature of the information and circumstances of disclosure.

2. OBLIGATIONS: Each party agrees to: (a) hold the other's Confidential Information in strict confidence; (b) not disclose it to third parties without prior written consent; (c) use it solely for the Purpose; (d) protect it with at least the same degree of care used for its own confidential information, but not less than reasonable care.

3. EXCLUSIONS: Obligations do not apply to information that: (a) is or becomes publicly known without breach; (b) was rightfully known before disclosure; (c) is independently developed; (d) is received from a third party without restriction; (e) is required to be disclosed by law, with reasonable notice to disclosing party.

4. TERM: This Agreement shall be effective for [2 years] from the date hereof. Confidentiality obligations shall survive for [3 years] after disclosure.

5. RETURN/DESTRUCTION: Upon request or termination, each party shall promptly return or destroy the other's Confidential Information and certify such action in writing.

6. REMEDIES: Breach may cause irreparable harm; each party may seek injunctive relief without requirement to post bond.

7. GOVERNING LAW: This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of India and courts of [City] shall have exclusive jurisdiction.

## Key Case Laws
- **Diljeet Titus v. Alfred A. Adebare (2006)**: Court protected confidential client database; breach of confidence actionable even without contract.
- **American Express Bank v. Priya Puri (2006)**: Post-employment use of employer's confidential customer data — injunction granted.
- **Zee Telefilms Ltd. v. Sundial Communications (2003)**: Disclosure of business idea in confidence creates obligation not to exploit it.
- **Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning (1967)**: Negative covenant in employment — enforceable during employment, questionable post-employment.
