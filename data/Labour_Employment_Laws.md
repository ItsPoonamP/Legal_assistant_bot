# Labour and Employment Laws in India

## Overview — Labour Codes 2020
India consolidated 29 central labour laws into 4 Labour Codes (passed 2019–2020, implementation pending full state rules):
1. **Code on Wages, 2019**
2. **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**
3. **Code on Social Security, 2020**
4. **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020**

Until codes are fully implemented, the original acts remain in force.

## Wages

### Minimum Wages Act, 1948 / Code on Wages, 2019
- Prescribes minimum wages for scheduled employments.
- **Floor Wage**: National minimum wage below which no state can fix minimum wages.
- **Components**: Basic wage + dearness allowance.
- **Revision**: Every 5 years.
- Violation: Fine up to ₹50,000 + imprisonment up to 3 months.

### Payment of Wages Act, 1936
- Wages must be paid by the 7th (up to 1,000 employees) or 10th (above 1,000) of the following month.
- Must be paid in legal tender (bank transfer allowed).
- Authorised deductions only: fines, absence, accommodation, advances, insurance, cooperative society.

### Equal Remuneration Act, 1976 (now Code on Wages)
- Equal pay for equal work for men and women.
- No discrimination in recruitment.

## Working Hours and Leave

### Factories Act, 1948 / OSHWC Code, 2020
- **Working Hours**: Max 9 hours/day, 48 hours/week.
- **Overtime**: Double rate; max 50 overtime hours per quarter.
- **Intervals**: 30-minute rest after 5 hours of continuous work.
- **Weekly Holiday**: At least 1 day off per week.
- **Annual Leave**: 1 day for every 20 days worked (workers); 1 for every 15 days (minors).
- **Hazardous Processes**: Special provisions for health, safety, welfare.

### Shops and Establishments Acts
- State-specific legislation governing commercial establishments.
- Covers working hours, leave, holidays, registers to be maintained.

## Employment Contracts and Service Conditions

### Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946
- Industrial establishments with 100+ workers must frame Standing Orders defining service conditions.
- Covers: Classification of workers, shifts, attendance, leave, termination, misconduct.

### Fixed-Term Employment
Under OSHWC Code, fixed-term employees entitled to same benefits as permanent employees on pro-rata basis. Gratuity payable on completion of 1 year (not 5 years as for regular employees).

## Industrial Relations

### Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 / Industrial Relations Code, 2020

**Industrial Dispute**: Any dispute or difference between employers and workmen relating to employment, non-employment, terms of employment, or conditions of labour.

**Machinery for Settlement**:
1. **Works Committee**: Bipartite body for minor disputes.
2. **Conciliation Officer**: Government officer who attempts conciliation.
3. **Board of Conciliation**: Tripartite body for conciliation.
4. **Labour Court**: Decides disputes relating to rights under standing orders.
5. **Industrial Tribunal**: Decides disputes relating to wages, bonus, hours.
6. **National Tribunal**: Disputes of national importance.

**Strikes and Lockouts**:
- Workers in public utility services must give 14 days' notice before strike.
- Employer must give 14 days' notice before lockout in public utilities.
- Illegal strikes/lockouts: No wages during illegal strike; employers not liable to pay during illegal lockout.

**Retrenchment (Section 25F)**:
- 1 month's notice or wages in lieu.
- Retrenchment compensation: 15 days' average pay for every completed year of service.
- Last in, first out principle.

**Layoff (Section 25C)**:
- Worker entitled to 50% of basic + DA for layoff period.

**Closure (Section 25FFF)**:
- Compensation equivalent to retrenchment compensation.
- Prior permission required for establishments with 100+ workers.

**Unfair Labour Practices (Schedule V)**:
- Interfering with right to organise.
- Discriminating against union members.
- Employing contract labour to replace permanent workers.

## Social Security

### Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952
- Applies to establishments with 20+ employees.
- **EPF Contribution**: 12% of basic wages by both employer and employee.
- **EPS (Employees' Pension Scheme)**: 8.33% of employer's share goes to EPS.
- **EDLI (Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance Scheme)**: 0.5% by employer.

### Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948
- Applies to factories and establishments in notified areas.
- **ESI Contribution**: 3.25% by employer + 0.75% by employee (for wages up to ₹21,000/month).
- Benefits: Medical, sickness, maternity, disablement, dependants' benefits.

### Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972
- Payable on retirement, resignation, death, or disablement after **5 years of continuous service**.
- **Formula**: 15/26 × last drawn wages × completed years of service.
- **Maximum**: ₹20 lakh (enhanced; subject to revision).
- Gratuity is not taxable up to ₹20 lakh for private sector employees.

### Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (Amended 2017)
- **Maternity Leave**: 26 weeks (for first two children); 12 weeks (third child onwards).
- **Adoption/Surrogacy**: 12 weeks.
- Crèche facility mandatory for 50+ employees.
- Work from home option may be offered after maternity leave.

## Contract Labour

### Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970
- Principal employer must register; contractor must obtain licence.
- If contractor fails to pay wages, principal employer is liable.
- Government may abolish contract labour in specific processes.
- Contract workers entitled to basic amenities (canteen, restrooms, first aid).

## Child Labour

### Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016
- **Complete ban**: Children below 14 years cannot work in any occupation.
- **Adolescents (14–18 years)**: Cannot work in hazardous occupations.
- Family enterprises and entertainment industry have limited exceptions.
- Violation: Imprisonment 6 months–2 years + fine.

## Sexual Harassment at Workplace

### Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition, Redressal) Act, 2013 (POSH Act)
- Every employer with 10+ employees must constitute an **Internal Committee (IC)**.
- Covers all workplaces including unorganised sector (Local Committee).
- Complaint must be filed within 3 months (extendable by 3 months).
- IC inquiry must be completed in 90 days.
- Conciliation is not permitted if the complaint involves a criminal act.
- Penalties for employer: Fine up to ₹50,000; second offence: doubled fine + licence cancellation.

## Key Case Laws
- **Bangalore Water Supply v. A. Rajappa (1978)**: Wide interpretation of "industry" under ID Act.
- **Air India v. Nargesh Mirza (1981)**: Discriminatory service conditions for air hostesses struck down.
- **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)**: Sexual harassment guidelines (precursor to POSH Act).
- **Workmen of Meenakshi Mills v. Meenakshi Mills (1992)**: Retrenchment compensation requirements.
- **Crown Aluminium Works v. Their Workmen (1958)**: Living wage concept.
