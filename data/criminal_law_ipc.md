# Criminal Law — Indian Penal Code, 1860 & Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

## Overview
The Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860 was the principal criminal code of India. It has been replaced by the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, which came into effect on July 1, 2024. The BNS retains most IPC provisions with renumbered sections and some additions.

## General Principles of Criminal Liability

### Actus Reus and Mens Rea
- **Actus Reus**: The guilty act — the physical element of the crime.
- **Mens Rea**: The guilty mind — the mental element (intention, knowledge, recklessness, negligence).
- Both must generally co-exist for criminal liability.

### Stages of a Crime
1. **Intention**: Not punishable by itself.
2. **Preparation**: Generally not punishable (exceptions: waging war, dacoity).
3. **Attempt (Section 511 IPC / BNS equivalent)**: Punishable — doing an act towards commission with intent, falling short of completion. Punishment: half the punishment for the completed offence.
4. **Commission**: Completed offence — full punishment.

## General Exceptions (Chapter IV IPC, Sections 76–106)

### Act of a Child
- **Below 7 years (Section 82)**: Absolute immunity — doli incapax.
- **7–12 years (Section 83)**: Immune if not mature enough to understand consequences.

### Insanity (Section 84)
Not guilty if, at the time of the act, the person was of unsound mind and unable to know:
- The nature of the act, OR
- That it was wrong or contrary to law.

### Intoxication (Sections 85–86)
- **Involuntary intoxication**: Complete defence if unable to know nature/wrongness of act.
- **Voluntary intoxication**: Only partial defence — intention is assumed but knowledge is not.

### Consent (Section 87)
Consent of the person harmed is a defence for acts causing harm not amounting to grievous hurt or death.

### Private Defence (Sections 96–106)
Right to defend body and property against unlawful aggression. The force used must be proportionate.
- **Body**: Right extends to causing death only if there is reasonable apprehension of death, grievous hurt, rape, kidnapping, or wrongful confinement.
- **Property**: Right extends to causing death only in cases of robbery, house-breaking by night, mischief by fire to dwelling/vessel.

## Offences Against the Body

### Murder (Section 300 IPC / BNS)
Culpable homicide is murder if:
- Act done with intention of causing death
- Act done with intention of causing bodily injury likely to cause death
- Act done with knowledge that it is imminently dangerous

**Punishment**: Death or life imprisonment + fine (Section 302).

### Exceptions to Murder (Culpable Homicide Not Amounting to Murder)
- Grave and sudden provocation
- Exceeding right of private defence
- Exercise of legal power
- Sudden fight in the heat of passion
- Consent of victim above 18 years (passive euthanasia)

**Punishment**: Life imprisonment or up to 10 years + fine (Section 304).

### Culpable Homicide by Negligence (Section 304A)
Causing death by a rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide.
**Punishment**: Up to 2 years, or fine, or both.

### Hurt (Section 319) and Grievous Hurt (Section 320)
- **Hurt**: Bodily pain, disease, or infirmity caused to another.
- **Grievous Hurt**: Emasculation, permanent loss of sight/hearing, limb/joint, disfiguration of face, fracture/dislocation of bone, dangerous hurt.
- Punishment for grievous hurt: Up to 7 years + fine.

### Assault (Section 351) and Criminal Force (Section 350)
- Assault: Making a gesture/preparation causing apprehension of criminal force.
- Criminal Force: Using force without consent to cause injury, fear, or annoyance.

## Offences Against Property

### Theft (Section 378)
Dishonest taking of movable property out of another's possession without consent.
**Punishment**: Up to 3 years + fine.

### Extortion (Section 383)
Intentionally putting a person in fear of injury to induce them to deliver property or sign a document.
**Punishment**: Up to 3 years + fine.

### Robbery (Section 390)
Theft or extortion in which hurt/wrongful restraint is used or threatened.
**Punishment**: Up to 10 years + fine; life imprisonment if on highway or during night.

### Dacoity (Section 391)
Robbery committed by 5 or more persons conjointly.
**Punishment**: Life imprisonment or up to 10 years + fine.

### Criminal Breach of Trust (Section 405)
Dishonest misappropriation or conversion of property entrusted to a person.
**Punishment**: Up to 3 years, or fine, or both.

### Cheating (Section 415)
Fraudulently or dishonestly deceiving a person to deliver property or to do/omit something they would not otherwise do.
**Punishment**: Up to 1 year + fine. If cheating by impersonation: up to 3 years.

### Mischief (Section 425)
Causing wrongful loss or damage to property.

## Offences Against the State

### Sedition (Section 124A IPC — suspended)
The Supreme Court in S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India (2022) stayed the operation of Section 124A. The BNS 2023 omits sedition as a standalone offence but criminalises acts endangering sovereignty under a new provision.

### Waging War Against the State (Section 121)
**Punishment**: Death or life imprisonment + fine.

## Sexual Offences

### Rape (Section 375 IPC / BNS)
Sexual intercourse with a woman without consent or with consent obtained by fear, fraud, or misrepresentation.
**Punishment**: 7 years to life imprisonment + fine.
**Aggravated rape** (victim below 16, gang rape, custodial rape): Life imprisonment or death.

### POCSO Act, 2012
Protection of Children from Sexual Offences — covers sexual assault, harassment, and pornography involving persons below 18. Mandatory reporting requirement for all persons.

## Offences Relating to Documents

### Forgery (Section 463)
Making a false document/electronic record with intent to cause damage or injury to public/person.
**Punishment**: Up to 2 years + fine.

### Using Forged Document (Section 471)
**Punishment**: Same as forgery.

## Criminal Conspiracy (Section 120A)
Agreement between two or more persons to commit an illegal act or a legal act by illegal means.
**Punishment**: Up to 6 months (if offence is not punishable by death/life imprisonment) or same as the target offence.

## Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) 2023 — Key Changes
1. **Terrorism** now codified in the main criminal code.
2. **Organised crime** provisions added.
3. **Sedition removed**; replaced with broader provisions on acts against sovereignty.
4. **Community service** introduced as a punishment for minor offences.
5. **Trial in absentia** introduced for proclaimed offenders.
6. Gender-neutral provisions expanded.

## Key Case Laws
- **K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra (1962)**: Abolished jury trial in India; provocation exception.
- **State of Maharashtra v. M.H. George (1965)**: Mens rea in absolute liability offences.
- **Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980)**: Death penalty reserved for "rarest of rare" cases.
- **Mukesh v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2017)** (Nirbhaya case): Death penalty for gang rape.
