# Environmental Law in India

## Constitutional Provisions
- **Article 48A (DPSP)**: State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment.
- **Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty)**: Every citizen shall protect and improve the natural environment.
- Right to clean environment derived from **Article 21** (right to life).

## Environment Protection Act, 1986 (EPA)
Umbrella legislation enacted after Bhopal Gas Tragedy.
- Central Government has power to take measures to protect and improve environment.
- Power to issue directions, including closure, prohibition, or regulation of industries.
- Sets standards for emission/discharge of environmental pollutants.
- **Penalty**: Imprisonment up to 5 years + fine up to ₹1 lakh; continuing violation: ₹5,000/day.

## Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
- Establishes Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State PCBs.
- Industries require consent to establish and operate (Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate).
- Prohibits discharge of pollutants into water bodies beyond prescribed standards.
- **National River Conservation Plan**: Covers major rivers including Ganga (Namami Gange programme).

## Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981
- Extends CPCB/SPCB framework to air pollution.
- Air quality standards set for pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NOx, SOx, CO).
- Vehicles must meet emission standards (BS VI currently applicable).
- Industries in Air Pollution Control Areas require consent.

## Forest Laws

### Forest Conservation Act, 1980 (Amended 2023)
- Prior Central Government approval required for diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.
- Net Present Value (NPV) of forest + compensatory afforestation required.
- **2023 Amendment**: Reclassified "forest" definitions; some exemptions for border infrastructure.

### Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 (Amended 2022)
- Prohibits hunting of listed wild animals.
- Schedules: Schedule I (highest protection — tiger, elephant, lion, rhino, etc.) to Schedule IV.
- Protected Areas: Wildlife Sanctuaries, National Parks, Conservation Reserves, Community Reserves.
- National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) governs tiger reserves.
- CITES implementation — regulation of trade in endangered species.
- Penalty for Schedule I offence: Imprisonment 3–7 years + fine ≥ ₹10,000.

### Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006
- Recognises forest rights of tribal communities.
- Rights include: Nistar (use of forest produce), cultivation rights, habitation rights, intellectual property over traditional knowledge.

## National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 (NGT)

### Jurisdiction
- Adjudicates disputes relating to environment, forests, and other natural resources.
- Enforcement of legal rights relating to environment.
- Giving relief and compensation for damages to persons and property.

### Powers
- Suo motu proceedings.
- Can order closure of industries, payment of compensation, restoration of environment.
- Penalty up to ₹10 crore on industries; up to ₹25 crore on government agencies.

### Principle of Sustainable Development
- **Precautionary Principle**: When in doubt, err on the side of environmental protection.
- **Polluter Pays Principle**: Those who cause pollution must bear the cost of remediation.
- **Public Trust Doctrine**: Natural resources held in trust by state for public benefit.

## Hazardous Waste Management Rules, 2016
- Classification and handling of hazardous wastes.
- Registration of hazardous waste generators, operators, importers/exporters.
- Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for certain categories.

## E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022
- Producers of electrical/electronic equipment responsible for collection and recycling.
- EPR targets prescribed (% of units to be collected).
- Registered recyclers/refurbishers must be used.
- Penalty for violation: Up to ₹1 lakh per violation.

## Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 (Amended 2022)
- Ban on single-use plastics (< 75 microns thickness) from July 1, 2022.
- Banned items: Earbuds, balloons sticks, candy sticks, ice cream sticks, polystyrene (thermocol).
- Mandatory use of compostable/recyclable packaging.

## Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
- Notification under EPA 1986.
- Projects above certain thresholds require prior environmental clearance.
- **Categories**: A (Central level clearance — MOEF&CC), B (State level clearance — SEIAA).
- Process: Screening → Scoping → EIA Study → Public Hearing → Assessment → Grant/Rejection.
- Public Hearing mandatory for Category A and most Category B projects.

## Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Rules, 2019
- Regulates development activities along coast within 500 metres of High Tide Line.
- CRZ I (Ecologically sensitive), II (Developed areas), III (Rural areas), IV (Water area up to 12 nautical miles).

## Key Principles in Indian Environmental Jurisprudence

### Public Trust Doctrine (M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath, 1997)
State is a trustee of natural resources; cannot transfer them to private parties for commercial exploitation.

### Precautionary Principle (Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India, 1996)
Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation.

### Polluter Pays Principle
Polluter must bear the cost of pollution; remedial costs recoverable from industry.

## Key Case Laws
- **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak, 1987)**: Absolute liability for hazardous industries.
- **M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997)**: Public trust doctrine; Span Motels case — hotel on river bank.
- **Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996)**: Precautionary principle and polluter pays applied to tanneries.
- **T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1997)**: Broad definition of "forest"; ongoing monitoring by Supreme Court.
- **Sterlite Industries v. Union of India (2013)**: Closure of copper smelter for pollution violations; EPL and balance with right to livelihood.
